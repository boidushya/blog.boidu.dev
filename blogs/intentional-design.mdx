## What is intentional design?

The short version: everything in Better Lyrics is there for a reason, and if we can't come up with one, it goes.

That doesn't mean it has to look plain. Better Lyrics is pretty on purpose and the animations are meant to be seen, but they're there because they make the lyrics easier to read and easier to follow along with, not because we wanted to show off.

If you don't know what Better Lyrics is, I'd highly recommend reading [my previous article on it](./better-lyrics) first.

## Designing for designers

This is a trap I've walked into more times than I'd like to admit. You build something, you get excited about a clever interaction or some animation you spent a whole weekend on, and somewhere along the way you forget that the person using it just wants to listen to their music.

Nobody opens YouTube Music thinking "I wonder how the lyrics extension is going to wow me today." They want to sing along! If the extension gets in the way of that, it doesn't matter how nice it looks.

## Reimagine, don't replicate

Better Lyrics doesn't look like YouTube Music. _At all._

I don't think respecting a platform means copying it. YouTube Music's lyrics are pretty trash, and that bugs me, because the app gets so much else right. So instead of blending in, I asked myself: what would the lyrics look like if someone at Google had _actually_ sat down and thought about them?

It doesn't look like Apple Music or Spotify either, and that's on purpose too. People get emotional about lyrics. Why shouldn't they look good?

![Every detail compounds](https://res.cloudinary.com/boidu/video/upload/q_100/v1759524390/bl/every-detail-compounds_aam977.mp4)
_Every detail compounds_

The looks are in service of reading, though. The gradient masks pull your eye to the line being sung, and the spacing stops a dense verse from turning into a wall of text.

Everyone has a different idea of what looks good, so Better Lyrics has themes. Some people want it minimal, some want it loud, and I'd rather hand them the tools than force my taste on everyone. It's _their_ YouTube Music after all!

![Themes for Better Lyrics](https://res.cloudinary.com/boidu/image/upload/q_100/v1759657870/bl/themes_axek4h.png)

## Settings are a last resort

I'll probably get some flak for this, but I think a big settings page is usually a sign that the designer couldn't make up their mind. There, I said it!

Better Lyrics does have settings. But every time someone suggests a new one, the first thing we ask is: why can't we just pick the right behaviour ourselves? Most of the settings that survive that question are there because people genuinely use the extension in different ways, not because we were too lazy to decide.

## Does this really have a purpose here?

That's the question the other maintainers and I ask before anything goes into Better Lyrics. Would it be cool? Do other apps have it? Doesn't really matter. It either helps you follow the lyrics or it doesn't.

It's killed a lot of ideas. Social sharing was one, because it does nothing for reading lyrics. Annotations were another, since they add a whole layer of UI without making the lyrics any easier to follow. We even talked about AI-generated song meanings at one point. Fun? Sure. Useful? Not really.

The stuff that did make it in passed easily. Translations, because plenty of people listen to songs in languages they don't speak. Click-to-seek, because jumping to the part of the song you want is the most obvious thing in the world.

Every feature has a cost, even when nobody sees it. It's one more thing for users to learn and one more thing for us to maintain forever, so it has to earn its place.

We've had reviews complaining that there's no onboarding, and you know what? They're right, there isn't any! That one makes me weirdly happy. You install the extension, open a song, and the lyrics are there. A lot of software has trained people to expect a setup wizard, so something that just works can feel a little suspicious at first.

## Performance is part of the design

Jank is a design problem. It doesn't matter how pretty something is if it stutters.

Better Lyrics runs every time YouTube Music loads, which means any slowdown is something people feel right away, and if it ever made the music stutter, even for a moment, we'd hear about it. So we spend a silly amount of time on bundle size and on how the extension touches the page. Are we performance nerds? Guilty. But mostly because nobody notices performance until it's bad.

Sometimes that means writing our own version of something a library already does. We'd rather make our own lives harder than make the extension slower.

## Guessing what you want

Click a lyric line, and you probably want to hear that part of the song, right? So that's what happens. No seek button, no confirmation.

If you scroll through the lyrics yourself, auto-scroll pauses so you're not fighting it. A resume button shows up if you want it back, and if you just leave it alone for a bit? It picks back up on its own!

None of these are a big deal individually, but put together they're most of why the extension feels nice to use.

## The features nobody sees

A lot of the work in Better Lyrics goes into things people will hopefully never notice.

When the synced lyrics API fails, the extension quietly falls back through other sources until it lands on YouTube Music's own lyrics, so you never end up staring at a broken screen. Newer versions go a step further and show unsynced lyrics right away while they keep searching in the background. A small indicator pops up, and then the lyrics start syncing on their own. Magic!

![Sync](https://res.cloudinary.com/boidu/video/upload/q_100/v1759542136/bl/auto-sync.mp4)
_Fallbacks you'll never notice_

The extension also loads in stages: the styling first, then the synced lyrics, then translations. If something breaks halfway, you still get something that works.

![Wrong Sync](https://res.cloudinary.com/boidu/video/upload/q_100/v1759545104/bl/wrong%20unsynced.mp4)
_Notice how the first line switched? Neither do most users._

And it works on phones. I genuinely didn't know mobile browsers could run extensions when I started building Better Lyrics. Spoiler alert: some of them can, and people love it!

![Responsive](https://res.cloudinary.com/boidu/video/upload/q_100/v1759544923/bl/mobile.mp4)
_Even the resume autoscroll button works perfectly on mobile._

This stuff doesn't make for a flashy demo, but it's a big part of why Better Lyrics feels finished.

## When we got it wrong

Early versions had a bug where the scrolling lyrics would drift a few milliseconds out of sync. You'd think nobody would notice a few milliseconds, right? People noticed. MAN did they notice. Syncing is the one thing the extension promises, so even a tiny drift made the whole thing feel broken.

I spent days on that bug. If we were going to sync lyrics, they had to actually be in sync.

## The boring part

Making something feel easy takes a ridiculous amount of work. Adalie and I have probably spent more time on scroll timing and backgrounds than on the features people actually asked for, and most users will never consciously notice any of it. I'm fine with that.

With over **30,000** daily active users now, there's always some pressure to add more stuff, the kind of features that look good in a changelog and grow numbers. I'd rather not. All people really want from us is lyrics that don't suck!

This isn't specific to browser extensions either. Figure out what someone is actually trying to do, get them there with as little in the way as possible, and be honest with yourself about whether each new idea belongs.

## Closing thoughts

People sometimes ask me about the design of Better Lyrics, and I never have a great answer. It's mostly a pile of small decisions made late at night, each one asking whether it helps someone enjoy their music a bit more.

Every now and then I find a Reddit thread where someone asks how to get lyrics on YouTube Music, and someone in the replies has already mentioned Better Lyrics. That's been our only marketing. It's the same reason you'd recommend a restaurant to a friend: the food was good, so you tell people.
